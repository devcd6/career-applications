**August 28, 2026**

Dear Hiring Manager,

Chronic disease outcomes rarely improve because a new best practice was discovered. They improve because a practice that already worked at one medical group reached the next one, and the next, before it drifted or quietly died inside a single system. AMGA's Best Practices Learning Collaboratives and Quality and Innovation Collectives are built around exactly that transfer, and closing that same kind of gap, between what already works and who actually gets access to it, is the thread running through every role I've held.

That understanding started at Sanctuary Centers, where I coordinated housing placement, one of the social determinants that drives so much chronic disease risk, for a caseload of 20 to 30 adults navigating multiple federal, state, and local subsidy programs at once, each with its own documentation standards and application windows. No single client's situation fit neatly inside one program's rules, so I built a standardized document checklist system that let us process requirements in parallel instead of one system at a time, cutting delays during our highest-volume periods. The lesson was simple: a good program only helps someone if the coordination behind it actually holds together end to end.

That instinct carried into research at UC Santa Barbara, where I coordinated participant engagement and data integrity across three concurrent labs, translating study findings into presentations and briefs for principal investigators and external stakeholders. Recruiting across real demographic range taught me that an outreach approach that worked for one community could land as intrusive for another, so building trust meant adjusting the method each time, not just the message. Good coordination depends on noticing where a process's default assumptions stop fitting the person or partner in front of you.

That discipline scaled at the National Council for Mental Wellbeing, where I directed daily operations across six or more concurrent workstreams for a 3,200-member national health association. I served as primary point of contact for that member network, delivered end-to-end logistics for 18 or more national conference sessions, including speaker recruitment, prep calls, and platform setup, and produced a monthly newsletter reaching more than 8,000 subscribers. I also assembled progress and funder-facing reports across those workstreams and built the centralized tracking systems that kept every deliverable on schedule. At that scale, the work was the same as it had been at Sanctuary years earlier, just moved up a level: national partners and a long list of moving deliverables depend on the same underlying discipline of keeping the pieces connected.

I'd welcome the opportunity to bring that same coordination discipline, tested across direct service, research, and national membership work, to AMGA's Population Health Initiatives and Health Equity team, helping the collaboratives, webinars, and reporting that carry your members' best practices forward run as smoothly on the ground as they do on paper.

Sincerely,

**Devika C. Dixit**
