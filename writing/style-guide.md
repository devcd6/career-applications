# How the owner writes (approved by the owner 2026-10-08)

This file records observed patterns in the owner's cover letters and longer writing. Facts about roles, dates and numbers live in `kb/roles/*`; this file does not hold facts about the career. For resume bullets, the wording is in `kb/roles/*` canonical lines and their bracketed alternatives.

## Cover letter structure observed across the letters
1. The opening paragraph starts with a general observation about how systems or services fail people, not with the applicant. Examples: "People's needs rarely separate as cleanly as the systems built to meet them" (letter:human-services-public-health-coordination); "Financial support systems rarely fail because the resources inside them are missing" (letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination); "Global health work rarely struggles for lack of proof that it works" (letter:nonprofit-marketing-communications). The "X rarely Y because Z" shape appears in 3 of the 4 letters read in full.
2. The organization is named as "the clearest example I have seen" of that insight, tied to a specific program or initiative of that organization. The role title closes the first paragraph.
3. A three-step arc follows, one paragraph each, in this order: Sanctuary Centers (housing caseload, five subsidy programs, voucher shortage), UC Santa Barbara (research, trust with different communities), National Council (national scale). Transition lines: "That understanding became concrete at...", "That instinct carried into...", "That discipline scaled at..." (letter:public-health-multistakeholder-coordination, letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination, letter:nonprofit-marketing-communications, letter:human-services-public-health-coordination, in variant wording).
4. A candor paragraph beginning "I want to be direct about one thing" names a real gap (no direct state-government experience in letter:public-health-multistakeholder-coordination; no disability benefits counseling in letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination; no global health organization in letter:nonprofit-marketing-communications), says what is offered instead, and states a learning-first stance. The phrase appears in 8 of the 10 letters.
5. The closing restates the arc: "The path from [direct service] to [research] to [national coordination] has taught me the same thing three different ways" (letter:public-health-multistakeholder-coordination, letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination, letter:nonprofit-marketing-communications, letter:administrative-program-coordination), then one short sentence of interest in the team. "I would welcome the opportunity" or "welcome the chance" closes 10 of 10 letters. Sign-off: "Sincerely, Devika C. Dixit".

## Pattern inside each paragraph
Each body paragraph follows the same sequence: an experience, a specific example, an observed problem, a lesson in one plain sentence, and a link to the employer's work. Examples of the lesson sentence: "The lesson stuck: coordination determines whether services actually reach anyone" (letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination); "Good research, like good service delivery, only holds up if it stays close enough to the ground to notice when something is not landing" (letter:human-services-public-health-coordination).

## Recurring phrases and contrasts
- A refrain used as a thesis: "Systems built in isolation from each other cost real people real time" (letter:human-services-public-health-coordination, letter:public-health-multistakeholder-coordination, letter:federal-healthcare-project-coordination, letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination, letter:administrative-program-coordination), once extended with "and sometimes a roof" (letter:human-services-public-health-coordination).
- Contrast pairs recur: evidence and implementation, research and practice, resources and access, policy and person. The "not X, but Y" shape carries many of them ("It was not a paperwork fix. It was a recognition that...", letter:public-health-multistakeholder-coordination; letter:nonprofit-marketing-communications and letter:human-services-public-health-coordination use the same shape).
- Altitude and distance language: "close enough to the ground" (letter:human-services-public-health-coordination, one other letter); the career is described as a move from direct service to research to national coordination.
- Numbers are concrete and one or two per stage: caseload of 20 to 30, five subsidy programs, six or more workstreams, a 3,200-member association, 8,000 subscribers, an 18-session track. The values match the KB.

## Verbs and vocabulary (counts over all ten letters)
- Facts are stated plainly in the first person: "I coordinated" 14 uses in 9 letters, "I built" 7 in 6 letters, "I directed" 2, "I managed" 3. Reflection introduces the lesson instead: "I saw" 6 uses in 6 letters, "I learned" 5 in 5 letters, "I noticed" 1, "I watched" 1.
- Signature words: "systems" 43 uses (all 10 letters), "coordinat-" 56 (9 letters), "translat-" 19 (all 10), "community" 12 (7 letters), "ground" 10 (6 letters), "fragment-" 5 (4 letters). Occasional: "implement-" 4, "infrastructure" 4, "stakeholder" 4, "access" 6, "nuance" 1, "cognizance" 1, "capacity" 1. "Responsive" does not appear.
- Words absent from all ten letters: passionate, team player, fast learner, dynamic, results-driven, detail-oriented, excellent communication, thrilled, eager, self-starter. "Excited" appears once (letter:human-services-public-health-coordination, in a sentence of interest in the role).

## Sentence and register
- Average sentence length per letter ranges from 26 to 39 words (letter:human-services-public-health-coordination 38.7, letter:executive-administrative-support 32.0, letter:public-health-multistakeholder-coordination 26.4, letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination 26.8). Long compound sentences with layered clauses are followed by a short sentence for emphasis.
- Letters run 500 to 878 words (mean about 700).
- First person singular, plain declaratives, one exclamation mark across all ten letters (the closing line of letter:human-services-public-health-coordination), no slang. The tone is humble about gaps and plain about results.
- Numbers in prose are often spelled out ("twenty to thirty", "six or more", "a half dozen platforms"); percentages and counts use numerals (35 percent, 8,000 subscribers).
- No em dashes in any of the ten letters or in writing sample 1; writing sample 2, a published blog, has four.

## Recurring lines and later editing
The refrain, the "same thing three different ways" closing, and the candor paragraph repeat across letters. They are the owner's own phrasing, reused on purpose, so a later pass for AI-sounding text may read them as repetition. Whether to keep or vary them in a given letter is the owner's call.

## Longer-form writing (blogs, briefs)
- Titles use metaphor ("Tending the Soil: Why Recovery-Ready Families Are the Missing Infrastructure of Addiction Treatment", writing sample 1). Openings use an image or a reversal ("The waiting room has always been the wrong metaphor", sample 1; "The clamor of the casino floor has given way to something quieter", sample 2).
- Sections carry short headings, a bolded key-facts list appears where useful (sample 2), and evidence is linked inline with citation links (both samples).
- A numbered set of principles ends the argument (sample 1), followed by a short closing image.

## Constraints that hold across the owner's materials
- Role titles: the NCMW title is "Project Coordinator" in every version; other titles appear as variants in `kb/roles/*`.
- No role is described as full-time or part-time in the KB.
- The KB carries no publication-status detail for the Recovery Ready course.
- Numbers, dates and credentials in letters match the canonical values in `kb/roles/*` and `kb/education.md`.

## Variations found (not conflicts)
- Letters differ on small scope wording for the same facts ("two to three concurrent labs" in letter:human-services-public-health-coordination, "three" in letter:public-health-multistakeholder-coordination and letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination; "four separate housing subsidy systems" in letter:benefits-eligibility-program-coordination, "five" elsewhere). The KB brackets these (A-sanctuary-centers-housing-coordinator-07 "four [five]").
