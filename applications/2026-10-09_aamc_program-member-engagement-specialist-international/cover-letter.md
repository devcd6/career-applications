Dear Hiring Manager,

Good ideas in health professions education rarely stay local because they are weak. They stay local because no one is responsible for carrying them to the next institution in a form that institution can use. The Alliance of Academic Health Centers International, the AAMC's international arm, is the clearest example I have seen of an organization that builds the channels for that exchange, through programs like the AAHCI Student Leadership Initiative and the International Advisory Committee on Artificial Intelligence. That is why I am applying for the Program and Member Engagement Specialist, International role.

That understanding became concrete at Sanctuary Centers, where I provided Housing First case management for a concurrent caseload of 20 to 30 adults and served as the primary liaison among clients, landlords, clinical teams, and 5+ external partner agencies, including local and state housing authorities and county behavioral health agencies. Each partner ran on its own forms and deadlines, so I designed a standardized document checklist that let applications move in parallel instead of in a line, and I recruited and directed 10-15 volunteers and interns for each open-house event that enrolled new clients into the Coordinated Entry System. Systems built in isolation from each other cost real people real time.

That instinct carried into research at UC Santa Barbara, where I was the primary participant liaison across the full study lifecycle and led recruitment and community engagement for demographically diverse participant samples through community-partnership outreach. I also prepared agendas, recorded minutes, and tracked action items for recurring lab and stakeholder meetings, and developed slide decks and summary reports for external stakeholder briefings. Reaching different communities showed me that an outreach approach that works for one group can fall flat with another, so the method has to change, not just the message.

That discipline scaled at the National Council for Mental Wellbeing, a 3,200+ member national association, where I managed member-facing communications and served as a primary point of contact for member organizations seeking technical assistance and resources. For NatCon26, I delivered end-to-end logistics for 18+ national conference sessions serving 6,000+ attendees, recruited and onboarded 50+ external speakers and partner organizations, and assisted with identifying topics and speakers for webinars and conference programming. I moderated national training webinars and community of practice calls, coordinated their registration, scheduling, and learner communications, and authored a monthly newsletter reaching 8,000+ subscribers. I also maintained shared content libraries on SharePoint and OneDrive and served as the primary post-meeting communications owner, producing same-day notes and tracking action items to completion.

I want to be direct about one thing. My experience has been national rather than international, and I have not worked inside an academic health center, with regional offices, or across time zones. What I bring instead is member association work from the inside, along with experience at ASEI managing concurrent cross-functional projects spanning national events, member campaigns, and digital initiatives across a nationally distributed volunteer organization. I would expect to spend my first months learning how AAHCI's Regional Offices and member institutions already work together before assuming I know how to move faster.

The path from a housing caseload to a research lab to a national membership association has taught me the same thing three different ways: members stay engaged when someone does the quiet work of scheduling, documenting, and following through. I would welcome the opportunity to bring that work to AAHCI's member institutions and the team that serves them.

Sincerely,
Devika C. Dixit
