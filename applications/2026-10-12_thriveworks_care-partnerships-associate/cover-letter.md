Dear Hiring Manager,

Referrals rarely fail because a clinician has nowhere to send a patient. They fail in the handoff, when the practice making the referral cannot tell how to reach the next provider, what the patient's coverage will allow, or what happened after the patient left the room. Thriveworks was founded by clinicians on the premise that effective mental healthcare should be affordable and accessible, and the Care Partnerships team is where that premise meets the referring office: helping partners navigate the referral process, adopt tools like the Patient Referral Dashboard, and remove barriers to patient access. It is the clearest example I have seen of an organization treating the handoff as work worth owning. It is also the same gap between care that exists and care a person can actually reach that I have worked on in every role I have held, which is why I am applying for the Care Partnerships Associate position.

That understanding became concrete at Casa Serena and then Sanctuary Centers. At Casa Serena, discharge coordination meant provider-to-provider communication, records transfers, and referral management, and I saw that a person's care continues or stalls depending on whether those calls get made. At Sanctuary Centers, I coordinated housing for a caseload of twenty to thirty adults with serious mental illness and co-occurring substance use, serving as primary liaison among clients, landlords, our clinical teams, and outside agencies including CenCal Health and county behavioral health. I also linked Sanctuary's co-located Integrated Health Clinic with our housing programs, scheduling and tracking preventative-care appointments so clients actually reached them. When a post-COVID voucher shortage hit, a standardized checklist let us move paperwork in parallel, and no affected client lost housing. A referral is only as good as the follow-through on the other side of it. Systems built in isolation from each other cost real people real time, and sometimes a roof.

That instinct carried into UC Santa Barbara, where I led recruitment and community engagement for participant samples that standard academic pipelines rarely reach. Outreach that built trust with one community could land as intrusive with another, so the approach changed with the audience, and the recruitment tracking tools I maintained showed whether it was working against enrollment targets. Outreach that is not tracked only feels productive.

That discipline scaled at the National Council for Mental Wellbeing, where I was a primary point of contact for member organizations seeking technical assistance across a 3,200+ member association, and the liaison for local provider teams asking for implementation help. I moderated webinars and office hours for CCBHC and FQHC professionals, which taught me that educating a provider office means meeting its questions and its time, not the presenter's slides. When I recruited 50+ external speakers and partner organizations for a national conference track, I adapted the pitch by sector: federal officials needed a formal ask and weeks of runway, corporate speakers wanted a tight value proposition, and clinicians responded to earnestness in the content. Your role asks for the same range, from physicians and practice managers to care managers and social workers, each with a different reason to say yes.

The rest of the job is the follow-through behind the outreach. At NCMW I maintained Salesforce NPSP data integrity, supported the reconciliation that fed leadership dashboards, and tracked six or more concurrent grant-funded workstreams to on-time delivery. I standardized workflows across the Growth team and partnered with Marketing and Communications on dissemination, so I am used to bringing the right internal team into a partner's problem instead of letting it sit between departments.

I want to be direct about one thing. My experience has been in relationship and coordination work rather than carrying a referral or sales target, and my Salesforce work has been Nonprofit Success Pack data, not a referral pipeline. I have not yet prioritized a territory of physician offices by referral potential, and I do not want to overstate what I know about payers and accepted insurance. What I offer instead is outreach tailored to each audience, a record of following through with partners, and comfort working from trackers and data, along with a plain intention to learn your markets, your coverage, and the Patient Referral Dashboard first and to ask early where I am unsure.

The path from direct service to research to national coordination has taught me the same thing three different ways: access depends on the people who stay on the line after the referral is made. I would welcome the opportunity to talk about how that could support the Care Partnerships team's referral growth and the new partnerships behind it.

Sincerely,

Devika C. Dixit
