## Company snapshot

- HJF is a nonprofit created by Congress in 1983 and named for Senator Henry M. Jackson; it supports USU and the wider military medical community. [unverified: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_M._Jackson_Foundation_for_the_Advancement_of_Military_Medicine]
- HJF describes its work as administering, managing and supporting scientific programs for military, medical, academic and government clients, including research management, staffing and financial administration. [JD]
- HJF's 2025 Impact Report lists three objectives from its founding legislation: medical research and education with USU, a meeting point for military and civilian medical personnel, and bringing other biomedical disciplines into its work. [unverified: https://www.hjf.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/HJF%202025%20Impact%20Report-FINAL.pdf]
- CDP is a training and education program at USU in Bethesda, Maryland that trains military and civilian behavioral health providers in evidence-based care for service members, veterans and families. [unverified: https://cdp.usuhs.edu/about/background]
- CDP was formerly the Center for Deployment Psychology; its own blog says it became the Consortium for Defense Psychology on 1 June 2026, kept the CDP acronym, and states its mission is unchanged. A search summary only, so older materials may still use the former name. [unverified: https://deploymentpsych.org/blog/new-chapter-cdp]
- CDP says it has awarded more than 99,000 CE credits since 2024, with training focused on PE, CPT, CBT-I and CBT for suicide prevention. Self-reported, from a search summary, not checked. [unverified: https://deploymentpsych.org/node/2877]
- CDP is funded in part by the Department of Defense through Health Affairs and is headquartered at USU. [unverified: https://cdp.usuhs.edu/about/background]
