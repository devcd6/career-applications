Dear Hiring Manager,

Community dollars rarely fail to help because the money is missing. They fail when the committee calendar, the agency list, the reporting numbers, and the people in need of service all live in different places and are held by different people. United Way of Tarrant County puts dollars into local agencies through its Community Fund, Veterans Fund, and Women United Fund, across community response, community health, financial empowerment, and education and learning, and pairs that investing with research and data. The Community Investment Programs Assistant role is where that work is held together day to day: the CI Cabinet and regional meetings, the minutes, the databases and shared drives, the community-level data, and the monthly, quarterly, and annual progress reports. A team that names that back-end coordination as a job in its own right is the clearest example I have seen of a funder treating it as part of the mission, and it is why I am applying.

That understanding became concrete at Sanctuary Centers, where my position ran through United Way and I coordinated housing placement for a caseload of 20 to 30 adults navigating serious mental illness, co-occurring substance use, and complex trauma, including Veterans served through HUD-VASH. I served as the primary liaison among clients, landlords, internal clinical teams, and 5+ external partner agencies, including local and state housing authorities and county behavioral health. Each agency understood its own piece well. What no one held was the whole picture of what was provided, to whom, and for what purpose. I designed a standardized document checklist so paperwork moved in parallel instead of one program at a time, and I recruited and directed 10-15 volunteers and interns per open-house event that enrolled 10-20 additional clients per session into the Coordinated Entry System. Systems built in isolation from each other cost real people real time.

That instinct carried into research at UC Santa Barbara, where I maintained recruitment databases and cross-lab tracking systems, prepared agendas and recorded minutes for recurring meetings, and collected and summarized evaluation data for principal investigators. I also led recruitment in communities that standard academic pipelines rarely reach. Good records, I learned, depend on the relationships behind the entries, and someone has to keep checking that they stay current.

That discipline scaled at the National Council for Mental Wellbeing, where I managed day-to-day operations across 6+ concurrent grant-funded workstreams for a 3,200+ member association. I built trackers measured against federal grant requirements, prepared and compiled results for progress and final reports to funding agencies, and tracked expenses across 5-7 vendors. I produced same-day meeting notes and tracked action items for committee meetings spanning 4 departments and 15+ external partners, authored a monthly newsletter for 8,000+ subscribers, and supported onboarding for volunteer fundraisers on national giving campaigns. The work was routine by design, and it is what let program staff spend their time on programs instead of chasing deadlines and version control.

I want to be direct about one thing. My agency relationships were built in Santa Barbara County, not Tarrant County, and my grant experience sits on the project side, reporting and tracking against funder requirements, rather than allocating funds to agencies or monitoring grantees. I do not want to overstate how either transfers. I now live in Keller, hold a Texas driver's license, and want to learn Tarrant County's agencies the right way: from your team, at the site visits and round tables, and from the agencies themselves, keeping what I learn in a resource list the next person can use.

The path from direct service, to research, to national coordination has taught me the same thing three different ways: resources only reach people when someone keeps the calendar, the records, and the relationships in working order. I would welcome the opportunity to do that work with the Community Investment Programs team.

Sincerely,

**Devika C. Dixit**
