# Insights Discovery profile: how to use it in applications

Source: Insights Discovery Personal Profile, completed 7 Nov 2025 (uploaded file, kept outside the repo; this note summarizes it and does not copy it). Personal data. Stays in this private repo.

## Rules for using it

- It is a self-report style guide, not a fact source. Nothing here is a KB claim. Never state a trait, type name, color or score in a resume, cover letter or application answer ("Coordinating Observer" and similar labels stay out of anything an employer reads).
- Show a trait through a KB claim instead. "Thorough" becomes same-day notes and tracked action items. "Systematic" becomes the standardized document checklist. The evidence does the persuading.
- The instrument is commercial and Jung-based, and its validity is debated. Treat it as one input. The profile itself says to check its statements with colleagues.

## Risks a people-facing role will probe, and the counter

| Profile risk | What an interviewer may see | Counter in writing and in the room |
|---|---|---|
| Low visibility, contributions overlooked, may underestimate own abilities | Understated answers | Lead with the result and the number. Keep hedges to one sentence. |
| Distant, cautious in relationships, clams up if not encouraged | Weak rapport with member leaders | Include one warm, outward act in each relationship answer: thank people by name, offer value with no ask, ask what was useful. |
| Difficulty expressing gratitude | Members feel unseen | Name recognition as a deliberate practice, not an afterthought. |
| Prefers not to confront issues, keeps important issues private | Problems surface late | Prepare one story where you raised a problem early. Say what you told whom and when. |
| Loses herself in detail, organizing thoughts can seem inarticulate | Long, tangled answers | Use a fixed frame: answer in one sentence, one piece of evidence, one result, one bridge to the role. |
| Reluctant to risk new ideas, slow to decide | Low initiative | Pair each process example with something you proposed. Use A-ncmw-11 and A-sanctuary-17. |

## Writing rules this implies

1. First sentence answers the question. Context comes second.
2. One number or concrete detail per claim, copied from the claim's metrics.
3. Name the gap once, plainly, then pivot to what you will do. Do not repeat the candor.
4. Prefer plain, factual wording. It matches the profile and the style guide. Avoid abstractions and generalities the profile says you distrust and others tune out.
5. Add one human beat to relationship answers (thanks, recognition, a check-in). It is the weakest area on paper and the one this role depends on.
6. Do not narrate your own quietness or thoroughness. Show it.

## Interview habits

- Answers are strongest when prepared. Rehearse aloud, 45 to 60 seconds each, using the frame above.
- A pause before answering is fine and reads as thoughtful. Say "let me think about the best example" instead of filling the silence.
- Start warm. A short opener ("I am glad to talk about this role") before the evidence helps with the distance impression.
- Expect extraverted, persuasive leaders in academic health center settings (the profile's opposite type, the Inspirer). The profile's own advice for this pairing: be enthusiastic and positive, give praise and recognition when due, do not dampen their ideas, and keep both flexibility and structure in the meeting.
- Stories the KB cannot supply (conflict, partner dropped a commitment, led without authority, disagreed with a decision): give them in your words and they go into the application's `proposals.md` for verification.
